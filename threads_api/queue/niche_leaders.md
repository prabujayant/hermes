# Niche leaders - steal these mechanics as-is (researched Oct 2026)

Imitate ONE leader's format per thread: copy its hook shape, structure,
and pacing, applied to our pillars (backend, SQL, interviews, leetcode,
career, AI coding, system design). Do not blend formats.

## 1. Matt Pocock (TypeScript/AI explainer threads, 250k+ audience)
- ONE high-signal lesson per thread, calm clarifying voice.
- Mechanics: relatable pain opener, then the concept in plain words,
  then the exact code/config that fixes it. Teach, never preach.

## 2. codingwithroby (learn-in-public with AI)
- Mechanics: "I am building X with zero Y experience" opener, then
  what the models taught, what broke, what finally worked. Learning in
  public beats lecturing. End with where the project stands now.

## 3. Sahil Bloom playbook (ICO hooks, open loops, evergreen)
- Hook formula ICO: Intriguing statement, Context, Offer of payoff.
- Open loops: promise the payoff, delay it across posts (cliffhangers).
- Skimmable: short lines, one idea per post, concrete examples.
- Evergreen topics only: content that still helps in 2 years.
- Always end with a takeaway post before the follow CTA.

## 4. Contrarian lessons (Notion co-founder style, 500k+ views)
- Frame: "most X advice is outdated, here is what changed."
- Numbered hard lessons from real production pain, no theory.
- Works best on AI-era topics: agents, vibe coding, code review.

## 5. Debate hot takes (highest reply rates in this niche)
- One strong opinion stated plainly, e.g. monolith over microservices.
- End with a direct challenge. Replies are the algorithm's top signal.

## 6. Cheatsheet / stack threads (comment-CTA converters)
- Numbered list of tools, patterns, or configs with one-line why each.
- NOTE: house rule overrides the usual comment CTA, the final post
  is always the follow CTA for @owlsky17.

## Universal rules across all formats
- First line must stop the scroll (dwell time is ranked).
- Concrete numbers, tool names, and error messages beat abstractions.
- Plain ASCII only: no em dashes, no en dashes. No post counters.
- Reply-bait inside value posts (a sharp question) earns replies
  that keep the thread alive in the 1h/6h reply windows.
